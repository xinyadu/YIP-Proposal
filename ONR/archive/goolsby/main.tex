\head{Problem.} Regional analysts synthesize news, public statements, social posts, and field reports that may be delayed, contradictory, or deliberately deceptive. After a typhoon, dozens of posts may report contaminated drinking water, although they repeat one unverified claim while a later field test finds no contamination. Standard retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) may treat copied messages as independent confirmation and miss the correction. The challenge is to reconstruct what happened while distinguishing new evidence from the spread of an existing claim.
\par\head{Central hypothesis.} We hypothesize that jointly modeling events and the reporting pathways behind them can make RAG more resilient to deceptive amplification. A system that tracks competing claims, source dependence, and corrections can seek genuinely independent evidence and preserve uncertainty, rather than interpreting frequent repetition as confidence.
\par\head{Research plan.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Represent contested events and claim provenance.} Extract event claims with actors, places, times, and source attributions; organize them into competing hypotheses about what happened. Construct a linked record of quotations, likely source copying, corrections, and retractions, using linguistic overlap, publication times, and reported origins. Preserve uncertainty about copying and event identity: shared wording alone does not establish deception, and repeated reports need not provide independent support.
\item \textbf{Develop evidence-aware retrieval and generation.} Retrieve reports for their independent support, timeliness, and ability to distinguish competing event accounts, not only their textual relevance. Train models with claim-level feedback on evidential support, dependence, and revisions so generated situation updates label claims as supported, disputed, or unverified and cite the basis for each assessment. When a consequential claim rests on copied reports, direct retrieval toward independent observations or corrections.
\item \textbf{Train against information manipulation using counterfactual reports.} Build a simulator that separates underlying events from independent observations and their subsequent propagation. Generate paired report streams with the same evidence but different levels of copying, selective omission, fabricated claims, and delayed corrections. Use these pairs to train retrieval and synthesis policies that resist changes in apparent consensus caused only by amplification, yet update their conclusions when genuinely new evidence arrives.
\end{itemize}
\head{Evaluation and expected outcomes.} Use \href{https://www.gdeltproject.org/}{\textcolor{blue}{GDELT}}, \href{https://trec.nist.gov/data/crisis.html}{\textcolor{blue}{TREC CrisisFACTS}}, and controlled streams with known claim origins and corrections. Compare against standard and self-refining RAG, focusing on the rate of amplified false claims presented as supported, fidelity to source evidence, and responsiveness to genuine alerts and corrections.
\par\head{Naval relevance.} During humanitarian assistance and disaster relief, Navy and Marine Corps planners must distinguish verified local hazards from rumors or adversarial messaging amplified online. Event-grounded retrieval that exposes independent support and unresolved disputes can strengthen regional awareness without dismissing legitimate early warnings. The simulated information environments also support what-if planning for contested information operations.

FaithScore (\href{https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.290/}{\textcolor{blue}{Jing et al., 2024}}) and FGAIF (\href{https://openreview.net/forum?id=Qhfw5CUVd7}{\textcolor{blue}{Jing and Du, 2025}}) provide vision-language grounding methods to adapt for claim-level textual feedback.
